# gameplay-stack--sim-crate

Brief · resumable · Epic `gameplay-stack-decomposition` (M2) · reads: `context/lib/development_guide.md` §Layering invariants · `context/lib/scripting.md` §12, §13 · `context/lib/networking.md` §Weapon placement is content · read at 3acc6d6

## Problem

Developer-raised, anticipated need. `crates/postretro` is a single bin-only crate of ~222K LOC, so every edit to it recompiles all of it: touching `startup/`, `input/`, or `main.rs` rebuilds the entire fixed-tick gameplay cluster, and touching gameplay rebuilds everything else. The cause is that the cluster has no crate boundary — `development_guide.md` §Layering invariants records why ("Collision lives in the binary. So do the fixed-tick gameplay systems… because they call into the binary's collision module"), and M1 removed the cycle that blocked the lift. When this is done, the fixed-tick core is a `postretro-sim` crate below the binary with collision inside it, and an edit to binary-only code recompiles no gameplay.

## Decisions

- **`postretro-sim` holds the fixed-tick cluster and collision**: `sim`, `collision`, `nav`, `weapon`, `movement`, `kinematic_mover`, `agent`, `agent_steering`, `combat_positioning`, `trigger_system`, `trigger_bindings`, `trigger_commands`, `trigger_pools`, `impact_policy`, `impact_effects`, `spawner`, `health`, `grant`, `fx`, and the fixed-tick half of `scripting` (below). The `scripting ↔ sim` cycle becomes intra-crate and legal.
- **The manifest cuts inside `scripting/systems/`, at the fixed-tick / frame-path seam.** `light_bridge`, `mesh_render`, `fog_volume_bridge`, `emitter_bridge`, `attachments`, `particle_render` and `presentation_cells` stay in the binary: each depends on `render-cpu`, `postretro-ui` or `visibility` — crates sim otherwise never touches — and `light_bridge` reaches `postretro_renderer` itself; and none is called from `sim` — their callers are `main.rs`, `startup/`, `session/`, `capture/`, `observability/` and `netcode/`. Moving them would make gameplay a dependent of the render stack, inverting the epic's graph, where sim is a **sibling** to the renderer over `entities`. They are grouped under `scripting/systems/` by the handler-wiring convention of `scripting.md` §13, not because they are simulation.
- **`hit_zones` and `mesh_anim` move with sim.** `hit_zones` is a `simulate_tick` parameter and `mesh_anim::animate_entity` is the render-free evaluator it calls. `hit_zones` reaches `postretro-model` and `postretro-render-data`; `mesh_anim` reaches those plus one `render-cpu` type at its install path, severed by the next decision.
- **Sim's workspace edges are the wgpu-free CPU crates below the renderer**: `entities`, `foundation`, `scripting-core`, `combat-model`, `net`, `model`, `render-data`, `level-format`, `level-loader` (`collision` builds from `LevelWorld`) and `lighting` (the light scripting primitives already live in `lighting::script_primitives`). Externals are glam, parry3d, serde, serde_json, log. Depending on crates the renderer also sits above keeps sim its sibling rather than its dependent — but name the cost plainly: a `lighting` or `render-data` edit will rebuild gameplay. Moving those primitives out of `lighting` would remove that edge and is not this brief's work.
- **The clip-table install path stays binary-side.** `ModelClipTable::from_metadata` and `ClipTableStore::insert_with_bounds` are `mesh_anim`'s only `render-cpu` coupling, both taking `&[ClipMetadata]` at level load. Sim exposes a constructor over its own plain clip data; the binary converts at the call site. Without this, one level-load entry point drags `render-cpu` into the gameplay rebuild unit.
- **`fx` moves whole and adds no dependency edge.** Its contents are emitter and fog reaction primitives over `entities` and `scripting-core` only — no wgpu, no `crate::render`, no `glam`. The presentation data the epic's open question assumed still lives there now sits in `postretro-render-cpu::fx`.
- **`scripting/systems/input_mode.rs` stays in the binary.** It self-describes as App composition rather than a subsystem output, and every consumer — `startup/lifecycle.rs`, `session/mod.rs`, `main.rs` — is binary-side. Leaving it behind dissolves the `input::InputMode` up-edge without sinking a type.
- **`TICK_DURATION` moves into `postretro-sim`; `frame_timing` reads it down.** The fixed-tick period belongs to the crate that defines the fixed tick. `postretro-net::timesync::DEFAULT_MICROS_PER_TICK` already carries the same 16,667µs for transport timesync; that duplication predates this work and unifying it would put the engine's tick rate in the transport leaf, so it stays as it is.
- **`resolve_weapon_placement` sinks to `postretro-foundation`**, with `legacy_weapon_placement` and `BASE_OFFSET`. Foundation already defines `WeaponPlacementDescriptor`, `PlacementOffset` and `PlacementRotation` and already documents the legacy fallback, so the resolution rule joins the type it resolves. Both sim and the binary then call down, and the netcode caller keeps working through M3. This does place an engine-owned content default one layer deeper than `networking.md` §Weapon placement is content frames it; the descriptor and its documented precedence chain are already there, so the rule follows the data rather than splitting from it. The chain is mirrored in SDK doc strings under `scripting/primitives/` — those move or stay accurate.
- **Spawn windup stops sharing netcode's constant, without changing its value.** `spawner.rs` reads `netcode::MAX_DELAY_MICROS` — the remote-interpolation delay ceiling, netcode policy — as a 250 ms gameplay grace period. Sinking it to a lower leaf would put netcode policy below the crate that owns it, which is the same objection that keeps the replication harness types in netcode. Instead sim declares its own named spawn attack-windup constant at the same 250 ms, documented as derived from that ceiling. A test asserts the windup is **greater than or equal to** it — `plans/done/E10--enemy-multi-attack/` records the rule as `attack_cooldown_remaining_ms.max(MAX_DELAY_MICROS / 1000.0)`, so the windup must cover the delay, not equal it; an equality pin would invite lowering enemy windup to match a transport retune, which is the silent gameplay change this decision exists to prevent. `MAX_DELAY_MICROS` is `pub(crate)` to netcode, so the assertion lives binary-side until M3 moves it. Behavior is unchanged.
- **Tests that reach binary-only code move to the binary.** Netcode lives in the bin-only `postretro` crate and stays there until M3, so there is no lib target for a dev-dependency to reach and these tests cannot stay where they are — the constraint follows from that sequencing lock, not from Cargo alone. They reach netcode harness items (the types `MovementOwners`, `NetworkIdAllocator`, `OpenAuthorizedShots`, `SeatTable`, `HostCommandQueues`, `ReplicableSet`, and functions including `produce_owned_snapshots`) in `sim/weapon_stage.rs`, `sim/touch.rs`, `sim/determinism_tests.rs`, `scripting/systems/ai_tests.rs` and `impact_effects.rs`; `crate::session::evaluate_pending_in_tick_impacts` in `impact_policy.rs`; `crate::input::{GameplayInputLatch, ActionSnapshot, Action, ButtonState, Binding, InputSystem, PhysicalInput}` in `sim/touch.rs` and `weapon/mod.rs`; and `crate::alloc_probe::AllocSnapshot` in `movement/mod.rs` and under `scripting/systems/ai/`. The harness types do not sink. `evaluate_pending_in_tick_impacts` is a thin composition whose halves both move, so that wrapper dissolves rather than relocating. Tests naming none of these stay put.
- **`alloc_probe` moves into `postretro-sim`, which gains its own test-only global allocator.** `#[global_allocator]` must annotate a crate-root static, so the allocation-guard tests cannot simply relocate; sim declares its own `#[cfg(test)]` static over the moved `CountingAllocator`, and the binary keeps its own against the same type.
- **The `gen-script-types` bin moves to `postretro-sim` and keeps its `#[path]` isolation.** It compiles exactly three `scripting` submodules to avoid linking engine-dependent code; once those submodules live in sim, the same trick works inside that crate and the generator does not grow to link the whole crate. The invocation becomes `cargo run -p postretro-sim --bin gen-script-types`, and the two committed-typedef test messages naming the old command update with it.
- **`crate::distinct_mesh_models` references are doc comments only** (`scripting/builtins/data_archetype.rs`, defined in `main.rs`). They retarget on the move; no code edge exists. Named here so the cluster's root-reach inventory is complete.
- **Visibility widens only where a boundary is crossed.** `pub(crate)` becomes `pub` for items the binary or a relocated test now reaches — the `simulate_tick` seam among them. A blanket widening of the cluster's `pub(crate)` surface is not part of this work.
- **This legalizes a cycle M4 must undo, and that cost is accepted.** `scripting ↔ sim` becomes intra-crate, so the compiler stops distinguishing the two: today a `crate::scripting` or `crate::sim` path makes a cross-edge visible, and afterward new AI↔sim edges accrue silently until M4's trait inversion. AI is the churn locus, so the window matters. Splitting sim in two now would avoid it, but that is M4's work brought forward against the epic's sequencing lock. Accept the window; M4 pays it down.
- **Non-goals.** Netcode stays in the binary (M3 lifts it) and AI stays inside `postretro-sim` (M4 carves it); both are epic-sequenced and neither is achievable here. No behavior, wire, scripting-semantics or SDK-typedef change. No combat-logic consolidation into `postretro-combat-model`. No split of the seven frame-path bridges into their own crate — they stay binary-side until something needs them elsewhere.

## Acceptance

### Automated
- [ ] `cargo build --workspace` and `cargo test --workspace` pass.
- [ ] `layering_invariants_hold` passes; `postretro-sim` has no dependency on `postretro`, and `postretro` is still depended on by nothing.
- [ ] `cargo tree -p postretro-sim` shows no `wgpu`, `winit`, `glyphon`, `postretro-renderer`, `postretro-render-cpu` or `postretro-visibility` in normal dependencies. `mlua`/`rquickjs` appear transitively via `scripting-core`. `postretro-ui` and `postretro-test-log-capture` are dev-dependencies only, which `layering_invariants_hold` does not count.
- [ ] No production reference to `crate::netcode::` remains anywhere in `postretro-sim`, and none to a binary-root item (`App`, `session`, `resolve_weapon_placement`, `frame_timing`, `input`, `render`).
- [ ] Touching a binary-only file leaves `postretro-sim` unrebuilt. Touching a `postretro-sim` file rebuilds it and every dependent, the binary included.
- [ ] The M15 Phase 0 determinism harness passes unchanged — same tick sequence, same event grouping.
- [ ] `gen-script-types` emits byte-identical typedefs against the pre-move tree, and `cargo tree -p postretro-sim` still satisfies the row above with the bin's dependencies included.
- [ ] Weapon placement resolves identically at both ends of the precedence chain: an entirely unauthored weapon still yields the legacy offset with zero rotation, and an instance-authored weapon still overrides all three lower tiers.
- [ ] A newly spawned enemy still cannot attack for 250 ms, and an enemy whose attack cooldown already exceeds that keeps the larger value.
- [ ] A test fails if sim's spawn attack-windup drops below netcode's interpolation ceiling; raising the ceiling above the windup trips it, raising the windup above the ceiling does not.
- [ ] The allocation-guard tests still arm and still fail on a regression — confirm by asserting a deliberate allocation trips them under sim's own allocator.
- [ ] Every relocated test still runs — confirm the filter matched, and that pre-move and post-move test counts agree for each file the move touches.
- [ ] No item is widened to `pub` without a caller outside `postretro-sim`.

### Manual
- [ ] `cargo run -p xtask -- run content/dev/maps/campaign-test.prl` plays identically: movement feel, enemy engagement, weapon fire, triggers, movers.
- [ ] Animated meshes still play their clips, and hit zones still register on a moving animated enemy — the clip-table install path changed hands.
- [ ] Lights, fog, emitters and particles still present correctly — the seven bridges stayed behind while the systems that feed them moved.
- [ ] A dev-tools launch (`--features dev-tools`) still starts and its debug UI still draws.
- [ ] A two-peer co-op session still joins, replicates and reconciles — netcode reaches down into the new crate and that path is unproven by unit tests alone.

## Path

Non-binding.

- **First slice: partition `scripting/systems/` in place, before any crate exists.** Move the seven frame-path bridges out from under `scripting/systems/` into a binary-side module, and sever the clip-table install coupling. This lands green on the existing tree and is the thinnest path that falsifies the riskiest assumption — that the fixed-tick and frame-path halves separate cleanly. Today only `hit_zones → mesh_anim::animate_entity` crosses; if severing surfaces more, the seam is not where this brief claims and the transplant should wait.
- **Then boundary-prep**: `TICK_DURATION`, `resolve_weapon_placement`, the spawn-windup constant, leaving `input_mode.rs` behind, and the `fx` re-export aliases in `scripting/reactions/{mod,registry}.rs`.
- **Then test relocation**, also on the existing tree: the netcode, `session` and `input` families. The allocation-guard tests are the exception — they need a crate root to annotate, so they move with the transplant that creates one.
- **Then one transplant.** E19's terminal renderer cut established move-everything-at-once over staged extraction: a partial cluster does not compile, so staging buys checkpoints that cannot be green. The rival — module-by-module with shim re-exports — trades a large atomic diff for a long sequence of temporary API surface, and the epic's execution model already rejects it. The transplant is the one-way step: reverting a ~100K-LOC move is a `git revert` and a re-ground of every later spec, which is why the three slices above run first.
- **Seams to investigate by symbol:** `sim::simulate_tick` and `simulate_tick_with_presentation_aim` are already fully data-injected and call nothing in `input`/`frame_timing`/`render` (`plans/done/M15--p0-headless-sim-seam/` formalized this) — the natural public face of the crate. `scripting_systems` is re-rooted through `#[path]` in `main.rs`; once the tree splits, the fixed-tick half becomes an ordinary module of sim and that indirection should not survive there.
- `crates/postretro/src/main.rs` is ~13.6K lines. This work only removes from it; do not take a split on as part of the transplant.

## Open questions

- Whether the seven frame-path bridges eventually become their own crate above both sim and the render-cpu stack, or stay binary-side indefinitely — **delegated**: out of scope here either way; record the shape the move leaves them in.
- Placement of `agent_diagnostics`, `mover_diagnostics`, `trigger_diagnostics`, `door_occluder_diagnostics` — they have no in-tree callers and may be registered only from `main.rs` — **delegated**.
- Placement of `sprite_collection` (`derive_collection_id` has production callers on both sides of the seam), `presentation_pool` (named in an `impact_policy` signature) and `runtime_movers` — **delegated**: follow the dependency direction that avoids a shim.
