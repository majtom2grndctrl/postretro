## Delegated answers
- **Seed salt** — proposed, confirmed at Task 1: the per-instance salt is the enemy's placement identity, not its `EntityId`. A map placement uses its authored entity index; a runtime spawn uses its spawner's placement index plus that spawner's spawn count. SplitMix64 mixes the salt with the descriptor name. The roll and the release draw draw from separate streams of that seed. Two same-descriptor enemies differ by placement, and spawn order changes neither. The choice and its test go in the Task 1 row.
- **Default hold range, recovery cap and reaction budget** — tuned on `movement-feel` under the lag profile at Task 8. Starting points: reaction budget 150 ms (floor 400 ms), default `holdMs` [floor, floor + 600], default recovery cap 600 ms.
- **Brute's new attacks, shared stance, speeds and graph nesting** — tuned at Task 8 and reported there.
- **Brute cuts and event times** — recut `punch_windup`/`punch_strike` near 167 ms and `smash_windup` at 433–467 ms at Task 8, with Blender 5.2 (`C:\Program Files\Blender Foundation\Blender 5.2\blender.exe`, not on PATH). Every `--trim`/`--event` goes in `license.txt`, and every moved cut is listed for playtest. **Owner to point to the source FBXs** (outside the repo).
- **Misspelled Luau handle** — strict globals in Luau data scripts: reading an undeclared global raises at load, naming the global and the script line. An agent's normal loop is `cargo check` plus focused `cargo test`, and shipped-content load tests already run every Luau data script. `luau-analyze` is wired into nothing (one hand-run script needs a binary path). This catches a misspelled handle, a misspelled `when` guard and a nil table field by the same rule.

## Owner additions (2026-11-16)

After the review loop the owner ruled on the charge stride and added two brute attacks, to build on this branch before landing.

**Charge stride: an exception, with a per-state cap.** The engine default stays 1.5x, the cap production content should keep. Test content may widen it: an animation state gains an optional `maxPlaybackRate` (default 1.5), and the brute's `run` sets it high enough for the charge (about 2.7x at 6 m/s on a 2.2 m/s stride). Sp3 records the exception. The owner may widen the default later.

**Combo punch.** A new tell holds the jab's windup for 666 ms, then the brute throws three rapid jabs. Engine: a release gains `repeat` (an integer, default 1). Its animation plays that many passes back to back and strikes once per pass at its moment, and the release ends after the last pass. One attack turn and one cooldown cover the whole release. Content: a short `jab_quick` clip cut from the Mutant Punch, played on a looping state so a client replays every pass from the replicated state alone. The clip is 800 ms a pass, so an animation state also gains `playbackRate` (default 1): a fixed speed for a state the stride rule does not scale, and the combo plays at about 2.5x.

**Leaping slam.** From within 15 m, a new tell crouches for 666 ms (the smash windup), then the brute leaps at its target, steering, while the Mutant Jump Attack's strike plays, and slams at the clip's `hit`. A target out of reach at that moment is a whiff. Engine:
- an attack gains `queueRange` (contact attacks only, at least `maxRange`, default `maxRange`): how far out its tell waits in line and requests its turn. `maxRange` stays the hit distance. This is the narrow half of the roadmap's "mobile stances and per-enemy reach"; mobile stances and reach re-checks stay there.
- an animation state gains `scaleWithSpeed` (default true). `false` plays it at 1x however its activity moves, so a leap's airborne clip keeps its hit time. The leap plays the jump on its own state, so the planted slam is unchanged.
