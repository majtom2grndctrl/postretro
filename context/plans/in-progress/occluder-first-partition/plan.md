## Delegated answers

- **Span sampling, blocking set, broad axes, slopes.** Every brush blocks: spans and depths are segment queries against a first-pass BSP of all static brushes (today's tree) and its exterior flood, so void bounds a span and no brush's class depends on another's (R6, R28 hold by construction). *(Revised in Task 3: one centre probe landed inside brushes standing on or abutting the face centre, so deep open space read `NoOpenFace` — occlusion-test floor 132 under block 140, walls 120/125 at their dividers. Each broad face is now sampled on a 3 × 3 lattice, `FACE_SAMPLE_FRACTIONS` = 1/6, 1/2, 5/6; samples whose probe starts in solid or exterior are skipped, the rule below runs at each open sample, and the face passes when strictly more than half its open samples pass. Same rule, measured at more points; order-free.)* The span is taken on each sample's line, offset a named small distance in front of the face, along each broad horizontal axis both ways to the first solid or exterior leaf. The depth test casts along the face normal from the same point. The vertical test casts down from in front of the face's top. Extents come from the brush's own frame, the minimum-volume box over pairs of its perpendicular side normals (R5). When axes tie within epsilon, every tied face is a candidate broad face, and the brush occludes if any passes (R18; deterministic, order-free). An axis within 45° of up takes the vertical test; any other axis takes the horizontal one. Reason: one tree answers every query exactly, and the first pass is cheap beside the bakes.
- **Clusters.** Touching means separating-axis distance ≤ 1 mm (`REGION_CLASSIFICATION_EPSILON`), over brushes exempt alone. A section is a connected set of members whose broad-face normals agree within a named angle. A member joins only when its extent along the section normal lies within the section's slab, so a pillar on tiles or a lamp proud of a facade stays out (R4). Coverage is the members' projected union, rasterized on a fixed grid in the section's own frame, over the bounding broad face. It starts at 50% and is strict (R12). The in-slab members' planes reach the occluder tier *(revised in Task 4: as plain occluders, window-jamb planes won early and cut the hall front to back, failing A7-over and A12. A member plane lying on its section's bounding box splits at occluder rank; the rest split only where no occluder-rank plane spans the region, i.e. inside the box, carving one cell and one portal per opening — the same ranking the door answer uses. `build_bsp_with_occluder_tier` takes `&[TierRole]` (`Exempt` / `Occluder` / `SectionMember(per-plane flags)`). Constants: section normals agree within 10°, a slab stands where members carry ≥ 0.5 of the face area within it, coverage grid 240 per axis, strict over 0.5)*. Reason: orientation and slab tests separate the street map's corners and fixtures without a locality term in plane selection.
- **Door recognition.** A pre-BSP geometric test on each mover's closed pose. It is a door when static solid touches (≤ 1 mm) both ends of at least one broad axis of its closed hull's broad face, and open space at least the floor deep lies in front of both broad faces. *(Revised in Task 6: two largest axes missed every warren door, 256×256×192 u blocks whose thinnest axis is vertical, so every axis's face pair is tried thinnest first; an end touch needs solid reaching past the end plane and stopping short of the mover's mid-plane, so a crate through a lift platform doesn't count. Parallel planes alone failed R8 on an exempt wall with gaps, so the door's other planes follow at a second rank confined to the door's slab.)* Only the hull planes parallel to its broad face feed the occluder tier at first rank, as non-solid splitters ranked after every occluder plane, so they cut only the occluder-tier leaf that holds the doorway (R7, R25, R8, R17: the door is never a blocking surface for classification). Sealing keeps `sealed_portal_ids_for_mover` unchanged against runtime portals. Reason: no two-pass rebuild; lift cars docked in a room touch only the floor.
- **id-51 lattice.** The lattice stays at `LATTICE_FRACTIONS` unless the measured sweep reports id-51 misses on coarse cells. Then it scales with cell extent, up to a named spacing, inside this brief. Reason: the brief delegates the decision to the measurement.
- **Solid and exterior sub-leaves.** Each one stays its own cell, as each leaf is today. Each occluder-tier leaf gets at most one interior cell, holding all its non-solid, non-exterior sub-leaves. Reason: this keeps every cell's hull convex for teleport hulls and fog masks; grouping is an optimisation the reported cell counts can call for later. *(Superseded by the owner, 2026-10-28, after Task 9i: one interior cell per connected pocket of a tier leaf, Task 9j; see §Block.)*

## Approach notes (Path, not contract)

- **Cell tree as the leaf → cell chokepoint.** After face extraction, buried lights and the full-tree exterior flood, derive a `BspTree` view with the same nodes, whose leaf children point at cell entries many to one. A cell entry holds the cell's hull (occluder-tier leaf planes for interior cells), bounds enclosing every member leaf, solidity, and the union of member faces; an exterior set comes with it in cell ids. `pipeline.rs` passes that view where it passes `result.tree` today, call-site edits only. Locator, fog masks, cell encode, face order, teleport hulls, AlphaLights, floods and cell visibility then read cell ids unchanged. Readers that need exact sub-leaf solidity (buried lights, face extraction) keep the full tree. Point classification on the view equals the full tree's, because the nodes are unchanged.
- **Runtime portals.** Generated at occluder-tier nodes only, distributed to occluder-tier leaf pairs. Each pair is then pushed through both sides' subtrees, keeping fragments whose two sub-leaves are interior, under the unchanged `MIN_PORTAL_AREA_M2` cull. One portal is emitted per pair: the convex hull of the surviving fragments, coplanar inside the occluder-tier winding. Hull over-inclusion only widens visibility. Dropping solid parts keeps doors sealing under full coverage, and no portal touches a solid or exterior cell (R3, R22). The full tree's portals still feed the exterior flood.
- **Plane selection.** Inside the tier, candidates are occluder planes only. The score adds a cross-section term: the area of the plane's slice through the current region, larger preferred, so the retaining wall's top beats its back face. Door splitters follow once no occluder plane qualifies. Below the tier, today's score is unchanged.
- **Baselines.** Until task 5 wires the tier into `pipeline.rs`, prl-build output equals main. Before-bakes, eye-pose records, leak, door-sealing and exterior-point references are therefore recorded from this branch just before task 5, with no main worktree.
