## Capture point and hemisphere
Every reflecting fragment lies on a horizontal plane, and the mirror direction of a view ray from above has positive height. For a capture point C at or below every bound plane, each box hit is at or above C, so the lookup direction from C has non-negative height: only the upper hemisphere is ever read. Hence C sits 2 mm above a plane at or below every bound plane: a derived group's own plane, or the lowest plane inside an authored box. A higher C breaks the guarantee (hits on the far wall between the plane and C look downward from C), and the horizontal-only offset keeps authors from breaking it. The 2 mm lift keeps C out of the fluid; a hit in that sliver gives a direction a hair below the horizon, which the shader clamps.

Horizontal position. Box projection needs only C inside the box; the box hit is independent of C, which is why an offset that leaves the box is an error. A derived group avoids a fit-then-group cycle: each face fits its own box from its own area centroid, and a group is a connected component of the box-match relation among one fluid entity's faces at one plane height. Matching within a tolerance does not chain, so a component can merge boxes that differ by more than the tolerance; components are chosen over first-match grouping because that depends on face order. The group stores the union of its members' boxes with its area centroid as C. Each member centroid lies in its own box, so the group centroid lies in the union, but not necessarily over water: surface pieces are brush sides minus solid (`environment_volumes/surface.rs`), so a ring around a pillar, an L-shaped pool or two pools across a walkway puts the centroid over solid. Such a C moves to the nearest point on the group's faces, which keeps the whole group on one probe. The fit encloses what the water sees: from a pool sunk below its room's floor, low rays hit the pit's sides, and a box fitted to them would project the whole room onto the coping.

An authored box's C is the area centroid of the up-facing fluid faces on the lowest plane inside it, not the box centre and not every face inside: a room-sized box around an off-centre sunken pool would otherwise put C inside the floor brush, and a box holding a main pool and a lower basin would otherwise put C at the basin's height but under the main pool's surface. Inside means area-centroid containment, the binding test, so a face straddling the box's wall counts exactly when it binds. C is fixed before binding, so a smaller box claiming the basin never moves it, and it still sits over real water. The same nearest-point fallback applies. The offset remains for a pillar standing in the water at that centroid. A box with no up-facing fluid face inside has no C; it drops in the first step with the binding-no-face warning, since that is the mapper's error.

Parallax error for objects inside the box (coping, pillars standing in the water, floor props) grows with the fragment's distance from C. A capture point on the water plane is closer to every fragment than a mid-room point, so the low-eye view across the pool — where the player looks at exactly those objects — has the least error. A mid-room C also places everything below mid-room height into the lower hemisphere, which a hemisphere store cannot hold; the two choices are coupled.

Full-sphere storage keeps a mid-room probe (glass floor, polished stone, a future world-material reader) on the same record: same tile, same mapping, same section. Water-specific parts of the design: the capture point rule, the up-facing binding rule, the fluid-only reader, and the Fresnel composite in the fluid pass. The record, the section, the stage and the light share are not.

A baked 2D "planar probe" per water plane was considered: a mirrored view is view-dependent, so a position-independent bake would need a 4D light field. The hemispherical capture at the plane is the compile-time form of that idea.

## Probe cap
Probes are layers of one array; the cap is the portable layer ceiling (`PORTABLE_MAX_TEXTURE_ARRAY_LAYERS`, `crates/level-format/src/prm.rs`). `content/dev/maps/stress-env-volumes.map` holds 574 `fluid_volume` entities, each with an up-facing face, and derived probes share only across coplanar faces of one entity, so derived probes alone can exceed any legal cap. No default test compiles it: `crates/postretro/src/startup/fluids_swim_surface_tests.rs` reads its map text for fluid names, and the only compile (`presentation_budget_measurement_tests.rs`) is ignore-gated and of a baseline variant. Inside-a-fluid drops may also leave it under the cap, so a dedicated cap test proves the cap; the stress map proves only that it still builds. Refusing would break an existing map the Problem promises reflects; dropping the smallest surfaces first loses the least visible water. Authored probes are a mapper's explicit request, so overflow there refuses. The same map overlaps a water and a sludge volume at one height, which is why the inside-a-fluid drop applies to derived points.

## Capture shading and SDF lights
The lightmap bake and the animated weight maps filter out baked lights authored `_shadow_type sdf` (`lightmap_bake`, `pipeline/lightmap_stage`, `animated_light_weight_maps`); the forward pass adds their direct per fragment (`rendering_pipeline.md` lighting map) for at most four lights, ranked by influence within the fragment's chunk window (`select_sdf_lights`); the rest add nothing. A capture built from the lightmap alone would reflect an sdf-lit room as lit only by bounce, and one summing every sdf light would reflect a hit reached by more than four brighter than the room. The capture applies the same selection per hit and traces a shadow ray per selected light against the BVH, the visibility class the bake gives static lights.

Indirect weight: the runtime scales SH indirect, base and delta alike, by `indirect_scale` (production default `DEFAULT_INDIRECT_SCALE`, 0.33) on world and fluid surfaces; lightmap direct, sdf direct and emissive are unscaled. A full-weight capture would reflect bounce about 3× brighter than the room it mirrors and skew each light's share toward its indirect. The capture bakes the production default, and shares are fractions of that scaled capture. The diagnostics slider then no longer matches reflections; a baked capture cannot follow a runtime knob.

Reference state: the base SH and the static lightmap exclude animated lights (`delta_sh_bake` stores unit-radiance bounce per animated light; `animated_light_weight_maps` stores their direct). Capturing with animated lights off would leave a room lit mainly by animated lights black. The cycle mean minimizes the reflection's excursion over the cycle. The mean is of the runtime's clamped evaluation (`render-cpu` `sh_compose::animated_light_scale` clamps the spline, then multiplies the clamped colour by the clamped brightness), so a strobe whose spline overshoots below zero has a mean the raw spline does not give. A light that starts inactive enters at its reference too; its live value is 0, so the ratio darkens the reflection until a script turns it on. A script-targeted light that already carries a Quake `style` curve keeps it at compile time (`script_light_membership` leaves an existing curve untouched, `start_active` included; it adds a placeholder only to a curveless light and rewrites `start_active` only on an `_animated` one), so it enters at that curve's mean: the capture matches the room until a script runs, and current_l follows any curve the script installs. Only curveless lights, script-targeted ones given the flat placeholder included, enter at authored colour.

## Load-time capture
Rejected first by `resource_management.md` §9 (no runtime render-to-texture for mirrors). It would also cost boot time and full-resident composes, and the animated lightmap atlas is valid only for the frame's visible cells (`rendering_pipeline.md` §7.1), so a load-time capture would read stale texels for every probe outside the first view.

## Light share
The capture is static + Σ_l unit_l × ref_l, all known to the compiler. For a lookup direction, light l's share is its fraction of the captured light; the reflection at live value current_l is the capture × (1 + Σ_l share_l × (current_l / ref_l − 1)), exact per texel. Storing that per texel per light is the per-light-layer non-goal; light share keeps the K largest lights and a few directional lobes (up plus four horizontal is the starting suggestion), blended smoothly so the ratio has no step at a lobe edge. The runtime descriptors carry the curve, not its cycle mean, so each share entry carries the light's RGB reference beside its roster index and lobe shares. Error is confined to lights beyond K (held at reference, logged as uncovered share) and to angular detail finer than a lobe. Per RGB channel, a reference channel of 0 means the light adds nothing in that channel, so its term drops out rather than dividing by zero; the clamp at 0 absorbs quantization when shares in a lobe sum past 1. A lobe that captured no light in a channel stores share 0 there: lobes blend, so one NaN would poison lit neighbours.

Per-light layers, the exact form, store one tile per covered light per probe: VRAM grows with the light count, combining them needs a runtime compose pass, and runtime-written probe textures read against `resource_management.md` §9. Light share stores K × lobes values per probe in one uniform instead.

current_l comes from the per-light compose descriptors in the SH volume group the fluid pass already binds (`anim_descriptors`, group 3 binding 11), evaluated as SH compose does. Script `setLightAnimation` with `startActive` writes the descriptor's `is_active`, so a switch reaches every reflection on the next frame regardless of distance or residency. Promotion crossfades a light between baked and runtime paths without touching its descriptor, so the reflection does not move, provided the fluid evaluation reads no promotion weight: `animated_direct_sh_compose.wgsl`'s `animated_light_scale` multiplies one in, `sh_compose.wgsl`'s does not.

Rival rejected: SH-ratio normalization. Each probe stores a reference tile (the SH irradiance at the capture point at the reference state) and the fragment multiplies by live ÷ reference SH at the capture point. It measures irradiance at one point, so a lamp lighting the far wall but not the capture point never reaches the reflection; it reads stale or non-resident rows when the capture point's cells are out of reach, so a vista pool keeps a switched-off light lit; the composed direct atlas subtracts promoted lights by their crossfade weight, so the reflection dips while an entity promotes a nearby light; and it adds a new SH sample-region consumer to `rendering_pipeline.md` §4. Light share has none of these and reads one buffer already bound.

## Fresnel and the camera
Schlick with F0 = 0.02: looking straight down F ≈ 0.02; at 72° (10 m up, 30 m out) ≈ 0.17; at 84° (100 m out) ≈ 0.59. Vistas across big water are where the reflection is strongest and where screen-space rays leave the screen, so the probe layer carries the vista look. No distance LOD is needed: the probe costs one tap regardless of distance, and Fresnel is the natural attenuation. Scaling F0 rather than F keeps Schlick's shape: at reflectivity 10, F0 = 0.2 gives 0.2, 0.225 and 0.661 at 0°, 60° and 84°, while a ×10 multiplier on water's Fresnel gives 0.2, 0.506 and 1, flattening the grazing band into a clipped mirror. The two agree at reflectivity 1 and 50, so only a mid value tells them apart.

The current fluid blend is straight alpha at the surface-over-fog coverage `a` (`fluid_fog_composite`), so a reflection added to the surface colour would be scaled by opacity, and clear water would not mirror at grazing angles. Union coverage `F + (1−F)·a` with colour `F·R + (1−F)·(surface over fog over scene)` fixes it; premultiplied output gives it directly, straight alpha gives it with the colour divided by the coverage in the shader. The collector drops draws at opacity ≤ 0 (`render-cpu` `fluid_surface/cull.rs`), so a reflecting zero-opacity fluid must be kept, after the fog brief's "draws its fog alone" precedent.

## Resolution and shimmer
A 128² full-sphere octahedral tile has 16384 texels over 4π sr, ≈ 1.3° per texel; the upper hemisphere's 8192 texels serve water. A 1080p pixel at 90° horizontal FOV spans ≈ 0.047°, so the probe is always oversampled and bilinear level 0 never aliases; no mip chain is needed for shimmer. The fluid surface has no perturbed normals (`VertexInput.normal` is flat per face; the wave is a UV warp), so the smooth style is a true mirror, and the pixel style's per-cell tilt is what makes the reflection read as water rather than glass. The tile size is an engine constant carried in the section header; 64² is viable under the aesthetic at 4 KiB per probe, so a later worldspawn key trading size for crispness is additive.

## Budget
BC6H is 1 byte per texel: 16 KiB per probe at the 128² tile. A 64-probe level is about 1 MiB against the 2 GB pool, 4 MiB at the cap, so whole-resident is cheaper than any streaming bookkeeping. The share table is one uniform, about 6 KiB at 64 probes with K = 4 and a scalar share per lobe; at the cap it must still fit one uniform binding (64 KiB), which bounds K × lobes × channels. Bake cost: 16384 primary rays per probe before supersampling, plus a shadow ray per selected sdf light (at most four) per hit, against 256 rays per SH probe over thousands of probes; the cold cost is the shading lookups, not the rays. Fluid pass: one extra tap, K descriptor evaluations shared by every fragment of a probe, and about 30 ALU for the slab test, lobe blend, Fresnel and composite. Bindings expected after the fog brief: fragment sampled textures 9 of 16, uniforms 7 of 12 (the fog brief adds a group 5 uniform), storage 8 of 8 unchanged; the acceptance row states the delta, so a fog brief landing other totals does not break it.

## Seams across volumes
A canal running through several rooms as one entity has one top face per brush; each binds its room's probe. At the brush boundary both boxes project the shared wall to the same place when each matches its room, so the visible difference is occlusion or brightness, not geometry. Lagarde's influence-weight blending removes the step at two taps and two slab tests near edges; a follow-up. A compiler warning when one fluid entity's faces bind more than one probe is cheap and belongs in the Path if the executor finds the step visible.

## Alignment with `fluid-ssr-reflections`
That draft introduces `render.reflections.style` and `surfaceReflectivity` (default 0.4, meaning a reflection strength). This brief lands both names first with `surfaceReflectivity` as a scale on Schlick's F0 (1 = water's 0.02, 50 = mirror), default 1. The SSR draft realigns its default and semantics to this brief before promotion; it also gains its off-screen fallback through this brief's binding without a cube type. The style switch and per-cell evaluation are shared code.

## Forward-pass door
A world-material reader needs the probe array in the forward pass, which has no free sampled-texture binding (`rendering_pipeline.md` §4, §7.3: exactly 16/16). Opening that door means freeing a binding there; this brief records it and does not solve it.
