# gameplay-stack--sim-and-netcode-crates

Brief · resumable · Epic `gameplay-stack-decomposition` (M2) · reads: `context/lib/development_guide.md` §Layering invariants · `context/lib/scripting.md` §12, §13 · `context/lib/networking.md` §Weapon placement is content · read at 3acc6d6

## Problem

Developer-raised, anticipated need. `crates/postretro` is a single bin-only crate of ~222K LOC, so every edit to it recompiles all of it: touching `startup/`, `input/`, or `main.rs` rebuilds the entire fixed-tick gameplay cluster and all of netcode, and touching gameplay rebuilds everything else. The cause is that neither has a crate boundary — `development_guide.md` §Layering invariants records why ("Collision lives in the binary. So do the fixed-tick gameplay systems… because they call into the binary's collision module"), and M1 removed the cycle that blocked the lift. When this is done, the fixed-tick core is a `postretro-sim` crate with collision inside it, `postretro-netcode` sits above it, the binary keeps ~72K LOC of orchestration and presentation, and an edit to binary-only code recompiles neither.

## Decisions

- **Both crates land in one stride.** The epic sequenced `postretro-sim` and `postretro-netcode` as separate milestones, and splitting them costs more than it saves. Netcode's only remaining production edge into the cluster is downward, so the lift is mechanical once sim exists; and separating them forces the expensive half of this work that a combined stride deletes outright (below). Sequencing is unchanged — sim still has to exist before netcode can sit above it — only the packaging is.
- **Tests stay where they are, through a dev-dependency cycle.** `sim` dev-depends on `postretro-netcode` while `postretro-netcode` depends on `postretro-sim`. Cargo permits this because dev-dependencies bind only test targets, and `development_guide.md` §Workspace already records that a dev-dependency edge does not enter the layering graph. Verified against a scratch two-crate workspace at this shape: a `sim` unit test calling a `netcode` harness item compiles and passes. This is what makes the combined stride cheaper — split across two milestones, netcode would still be bin-only at M2 and the harness-reaching tests in `sim/weapon_stage.rs`, `sim/touch.rs`, `sim/determinism_tests.rs`, `scripting/systems/ai_tests.rs` and `impact_effects.rs` would have to relocate to the binary and reach sim through a widened public API. None of that happens now.
- **`postretro-sim` holds the fixed-tick cluster and collision**: `sim`, `collision`, `nav`, `weapon`, `movement`, `kinematic_mover`, `agent`, `agent_steering`, `combat_positioning`, `trigger_system`, `trigger_bindings`, `trigger_commands`, `trigger_pools`, `impact_policy`, `impact_effects`, `spawner`, `health`, `grant`, `fx`, and the fixed-tick half of `scripting` (below). The `scripting ↔ sim` cycle becomes intra-crate and legal.
- **The manifest cuts inside `scripting/systems/`, at the fixed-tick / frame-path seam.** `light_bridge`, `mesh_render`, `fog_volume_bridge`, `emitter_bridge`, `attachments`, `particle_render` and `presentation_cells` stay in the binary: each depends on `render-cpu`, `postretro-ui` or `visibility` — crates sim otherwise never touches — and `light_bridge` reaches `postretro_renderer` itself; none is called from `sim`, their callers being `main.rs`, `startup/`, `session/`, `capture/`, `observability/` and `netcode/`. Moving them would make gameplay a dependent of the render stack, inverting the epic's graph, where sim is a **sibling** to the renderer over `entities`. They sit under `scripting/systems/` by the handler-wiring convention of `scripting.md` §13, not because they are simulation.
- **`hit_zones` and `mesh_anim` move with sim.** `hit_zones` is a `simulate_tick` parameter and `mesh_anim::animate_entity` is the render-free evaluator it calls. `hit_zones` reaches `postretro-model` and `postretro-render-data`; `mesh_anim` reaches those plus one `render-cpu` type at its install path, severed below.
- **The clip-table install path stays binary-side.** `ModelClipTable::from_metadata` and `ClipTableStore::insert_with_bounds` are `mesh_anim`'s only `render-cpu` coupling, both taking `&[ClipMetadata]` at level load. Sim exposes a constructor over its own plain clip data; the binary converts at the call site. Without this, one level-load entry point drags `render-cpu` into the gameplay rebuild unit.
- **Sim's workspace edges are the wgpu-free CPU crates below the renderer**: `entities`, `foundation`, `scripting-core`, `combat-model`, `net`, `model`, `render-data`, `level-format`, `level-loader` (`collision` builds from `LevelWorld`) and `lighting` (the light scripting primitives already live in `lighting::script_primitives`). Externals are glam, parry3d, serde, serde_json, log. Depending on crates the renderer also sits above keeps sim its sibling rather than its dependent — but name the cost plainly: a `lighting` or `render-data` edit will rebuild gameplay. Moving those primitives out of `lighting` would remove that edge and is not this brief's work.
- **`postretro-netcode` takes `netcode/` whole**, over `postretro-sim`, `net`, `entities`, `foundation`, `scripting-core`, `combat-model` and `model`, with glam, parry3d, thiserror and serde. Its `render-cpu`, `visibility` and `lighting` references are all inside `projectile_presentation.rs`'s test module and become dev-dependencies. It keeps sole ownership of registry replication (`networking.md`).
- **Netcode's reach into the binary root is boundary-prep**: `App`, `session`, `clear_released_seat_slot_values`, `resolve_map_path`, `resolve_mesh_entity_bindings_for_entities`, `resolve_weapon_placement`, `presentation_pool`, `startup` and `frame_timing`. Each either sinks with its owner, inverts to a parameter, or stays binary-side with the call site moving up. `resolve_weapon_placement` and `frame_timing::TICK_DURATION` are already handled below; the rest are decided at build time against the live tree.
- **`fx` moves whole and adds no dependency edge.** Its contents are emitter and fog reaction primitives over `entities` and `scripting-core` only — no wgpu, no `crate::render`, no `glam`. The presentation data the epic's open question assumed still lives there now sits in `postretro-render-cpu::fx`.
- **`scripting/systems/input_mode.rs` stays in the binary.** It self-describes as App composition rather than a subsystem output, and every consumer — `startup/lifecycle.rs`, `session/mod.rs`, `main.rs` — is binary-side. Leaving it behind dissolves the `input::InputMode` up-edge without sinking a type.
- **`TICK_DURATION` moves into `postretro-sim`; `frame_timing` reads it down.** The fixed-tick period belongs to the crate that defines the fixed tick. `postretro-net::timesync::DEFAULT_MICROS_PER_TICK` already carries the same 16,667µs for transport timesync; that duplication predates this work and unifying it would put the engine's tick rate in the transport leaf, so it stays as it is.
- **`resolve_weapon_placement` sinks to `postretro-foundation`**, with `legacy_weapon_placement` and `BASE_OFFSET`. Foundation already defines `WeaponPlacementDescriptor`, `PlacementOffset` and `PlacementRotation` and already documents the legacy fallback, so the resolution rule joins the type it resolves, and sim, netcode and the binary all call down. This places an engine-owned content default one layer deeper than `networking.md` §Weapon placement is content frames it; the descriptor and its documented precedence chain are already there, so the rule follows the data rather than splitting from it. The chain is mirrored in SDK doc strings under `scripting/primitives/` — those move or stay accurate.
- **Spawn windup stops sharing netcode's constant, without changing its value.** `spawner.rs` reads `netcode::MAX_DELAY_MICROS` — the remote-interpolation delay ceiling, netcode policy — as a 250 ms gameplay grace period, and sim sits below netcode so the edge cannot survive. Sim declares its own named spawn attack-windup constant at the same 250 ms, documented as derived from that ceiling. A test in `postretro-netcode`, which sees both, asserts the windup is **greater than or equal to** it: `plans/done/E10--enemy-multi-attack/` records the rule as `attack_cooldown_remaining_ms.max(MAX_DELAY_MICROS / 1000.0)`, so the windup must cover the delay, not equal it, and an equality pin would invite lowering enemy windup to match a transport retune. Behavior is unchanged.
- **`alloc_probe` moves into `postretro-sim`, which gains its own test-only global allocator.** `#[global_allocator]` must annotate a crate-root static, so sim declares its own `#[cfg(test)]` static over the moved `CountingAllocator` and the binary keeps its own against the same type.
- **`crate::distinct_mesh_models` references are doc comments only** (`scripting/builtins/data_archetype.rs`, defined in `main.rs`). They retarget on the move; no code edge exists. Named so the cluster's root-reach inventory is complete.
- **The `gen-script-types` bin moves to `postretro-sim` and keeps its `#[path]` isolation.** It compiles exactly three `scripting` submodules to avoid linking engine-dependent code; once those live in sim, the same trick works inside that crate and the generator does not grow to link the whole crate. The invocation becomes `cargo run -p postretro-sim --bin gen-script-types`, and the two committed-typedef test messages naming the old command update with it.
- **Visibility widens only where a boundary is crossed.** `pub(crate)` becomes `pub` for items another crate now reaches — the `simulate_tick` seam among them. A blanket widening of either cluster's `pub(crate)` surface is not part of this work.
- **This legalizes a cycle M4 must undo, and that cost is accepted.** `scripting ↔ sim` becomes intra-crate, so the compiler stops distinguishing the two: today a `crate::scripting` or `crate::sim` path makes a cross-edge visible, and afterward new AI↔sim edges accrue silently until M4's trait inversion. AI is the churn locus, so the window matters. Splitting sim in two now would avoid it, but that is M4's work brought forward against the epic's sequencing lock. Accept the window; M4 pays it down.
- **Non-goals.** AI stays inside `postretro-sim`; M4 carves it out, and **the AI edit loop does not improve here** — netcode depends on sim, AI lives in sim, and Cargo rebuilds every dependent of a changed crate, so an AI edit still rebuilds netcode. What lands is the binary-only loop. No behavior, wire, scripting-semantics or SDK-typedef change. No combat-logic consolidation into `postretro-combat-model`. No split of the seven frame-path bridges into their own crate.

## Acceptance

### Automated
- [ ] `cargo build --workspace` and `cargo test --workspace` pass.
- [ ] `layering_invariants_hold` passes; neither new crate depends on `postretro`, and `postretro` is still depended on by nothing.
- [ ] `cargo tree -p postretro-sim` shows no `wgpu`, `winit`, `glyphon`, `postretro-renderer`, `postretro-render-cpu`, `postretro-visibility` or `postretro-netcode` in normal dependencies. `mlua`/`rquickjs` appear transitively via `scripting-core`.
- [ ] `cargo tree -p postretro-netcode` shows no `wgpu`, `winit` or `glyphon`, and shows `postretro-sim`.
- [ ] The dev-dependency cycle holds: `postretro-sim`'s test targets link `postretro-netcode`, and `cargo build -p postretro-sim` (no tests) does not.
- [ ] No production reference to a binary-root item (`App`, `session`, `resolve_weapon_placement`, `frame_timing`, `input`, `render`, `presentation_pool`) remains in either crate.
- [ ] Touching a binary-only file leaves both crates unrebuilt. Touching a `postretro-sim` file rebuilds it, `postretro-netcode`, and the binary.
- [ ] The M15 Phase 0 determinism harness passes unchanged — same tick sequence, same event grouping.
- [ ] `gen-script-types` emits byte-identical typedefs against the pre-move tree.
- [ ] Weapon placement resolves identically at both ends of the precedence chain: an entirely unauthored weapon still yields the legacy offset with zero rotation, and an instance-authored weapon still overrides all three lower tiers.
- [ ] A newly spawned enemy still cannot attack for 250 ms, and an enemy whose attack cooldown already exceeds that keeps the larger value.
- [ ] A test fails if sim's spawn attack-windup drops below netcode's interpolation ceiling; raising the ceiling above the windup trips it, raising the windup above the ceiling does not.
- [ ] The allocation-guard tests still arm and still fail on a regression — confirm by asserting a deliberate allocation trips them under sim's own allocator.
- [ ] Pre-move and post-move test counts agree per file for every file either crate takes.
- [ ] No item is widened to `pub` without a caller outside its crate.

### Manual
- [ ] `cargo run -p xtask -- run content/dev/maps/campaign-test.prl` plays identically: movement feel, enemy engagement, weapon fire, triggers, movers.
- [ ] Animated meshes still play their clips, and hit zones still register on a moving animated enemy — the clip-table install path changed hands.
- [ ] Lights, fog, emitters and particles still present correctly — the seven bridges stayed behind while the systems that feed them moved.
- [ ] A dev-tools launch (`--features dev-tools`) still starts and its debug UI still draws.
- [ ] A two-peer co-op session joins, replicates, reconciles, and survives a host level change — netcode crossed a crate boundary and its seat/slot lifecycle is unproven by unit tests alone.

## Path

Non-binding.

- **First slice: partition `scripting/systems/` in place, before either crate exists.** Move the seven frame-path bridges out from under `scripting/systems/` into a binary-side module, and sever the clip-table install coupling. This lands green on the existing tree and is the thinnest path that falsifies the riskiest assumption — that the fixed-tick and frame-path halves separate cleanly. Today only `hit_zones → mesh_anim::animate_entity` crosses; if severing surfaces more, the seam is not where this brief claims.
- **Then boundary-prep for both crates**: `TICK_DURATION`, `resolve_weapon_placement`, the spawn-windup constant, leaving `input_mode.rs` behind, the `fx` re-export aliases in `scripting/reactions/{mod,registry}.rs`, and netcode's nine binary-root reach-ins. All of it lands on the existing tree.
- **Then both transplants, sim first.** E19's terminal renderer cut established move-everything-at-once over staged extraction: a partial cluster does not compile, so staging buys checkpoints that cannot be green. Netcode follows immediately — it is a near-mechanical lift once sim exists, and the dev-dependency cycle that keeps sim's tests in place needs it. The allocation-guard tests and sim's `#[cfg(test)]` allocator land with the sim transplant, since they need a crate root to annotate.
- **Seams to investigate by symbol:** `sim::simulate_tick` and `simulate_tick_with_presentation_aim` are already fully data-injected and call nothing in `input`/`frame_timing`/`render` (`plans/done/M15--p0-headless-sim-seam/` formalized this) — the natural public face of sim. `scripting_systems` is re-rooted through `#[path]` in `main.rs`; once the tree splits, the fixed-tick half becomes an ordinary module of sim and that indirection should not survive there.
- `crates/postretro/src/main.rs` is ~13.6K lines. This work only removes from it; do not take a split on as part of the transplants.

## Open questions

- Whether the seven frame-path bridges eventually become their own crate above both sim and the render-cpu stack, or stay binary-side indefinitely — **delegated**: out of scope either way; record the shape the move leaves them in.
- Placement of `agent_diagnostics`, `mover_diagnostics`, `trigger_diagnostics`, `door_occluder_diagnostics` — they have no in-tree callers and may be registered only from `main.rs` — **delegated**.
- Placement of `sprite_collection` (`derive_collection_id` has production callers on both sides of the seam), `presentation_pool` (named in an `impact_policy` signature and reached from netcode) and `runtime_movers` — **delegated**: follow the dependency direction that avoids a shim.
