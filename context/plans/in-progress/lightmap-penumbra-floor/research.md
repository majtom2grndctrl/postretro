# lightmap-penumbra-floor — research

Read at a0a2c0239.

## Why an emitter-size floor fails

The roadmap's first framing raised each light's effective emitter size to span ~2 texels at the lit surface. Penumbra width at a receiver is roughly emitter size × occluder-to-receiver distance ÷ light-to-occluder distance. At contact, occluder-to-receiver distance is zero, so the edge is hard whatever the emitter size. `soft_visibility` traces boolean rays and never learns hit distance, so the bake cannot size a per-texel emitter either. `lightmap-resolution/research.md` measured "~1 texel at contact" at 0.04 m with the default 0.25 m light. A receiver footprint fixes edge width regardless of light size or contact.

## Footprint kernel

Model: a straight hard edge on an integer texel grid, kernels integrated exactly, bilinear reconstruction sampled densely, angles 0–45° at sixteen sub-texel phases each. Ramps and wobble are in texels; differences are in visibility.

| Kernel | Reach | Continuous 10–90 ramp | 10–90 ramp after bilinear | Staircase wobble (iso-0.5, peak to peak) | Largest adjacent stored difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Point (today) | 0 | 0 | 0.80–1.54 | 0.91 | 1.00 |
| Box, 1 texel | 0.71 (corner) | 0.78–0.80 | 0.80–1.60 | 0.17 | 1.00 |
| **Round, radius 1** | 1 | 1.04 | 1.11–1.66 | 0.14 | 0.78 |
| Tent, radius 1 (separable, bilinear's own) | 1.41 (diagonal) | 1.07–1.11 | 1.18–1.70 | 0.14 | 0.75 |
| Round, radius 1.5 | 1.5 | 1.56 | 1.69–2.06 | 0.07 | 0.57 |

- **Why round, radius 1.** It cuts the point sample's staircase as far as the separable tent does, and its reach is exactly one texel in every direction. The tent reaches √2 along diagonals, so texels just past one texel of a 45° edge store fractions where the exact-0/1 rows expect 0 or 1. A one-texel box is narrower but still stores a 0/1 pair across a mid-phase edge, so it keeps the step. Radius 1.5 halves the wobble for half a texel more blur on every hard edge, which is the owner's call.
- **Ramp depends on phase and angle.** A round kernel's profile across an edge is the same at every angle, so its continuous ramp is one number. After bilinear the ramp varies with where the edge falls between texel centres and with its angle, so the rows compare a bake against the analytic ramp at the same phase and angle.
- **Largest adjacent difference.** For any occluder pattern, two texels' exact filtered visibility differ by at most the part of one kernel's weight that exceeds the other's, and a half-plane on their bisector attains it. For axis neighbours under the round radius-1 kernel that is 0.78, from a mid-phase axis-aligned edge with texels at 0.11 and 0.89; diagonal neighbours reach 0.94. Every hard 0/1 step contains an axis-adjacent 0/1 pair, so axis neighbours suffice, and a threshold of 0.78 plus a margin under 0.22 always flags a hard step. A thin strip obeys the same bound, so railings and wires never warn with the floor on.
- **Sampling margin.** With the probe early-out, and the four probes a quarter-turn ring of radius at least 0.72 texel inside a cone-distributed set, a floored bake's worst axis-adjacent difference on straight edges and thin strips is 0.750 at 4 samples, 0.800 at 5, 0.833 at 6, 0.857 at 7 and 0.875 from 8 to 64. With probes not so placed, 0/1 pairs remain on most edges at every count above 4. So the margin sits between about 0.10 (0.875 − 0.780) and just under 0.22 (a hard step is 1.0), and the probe ring is part of the design; P16 pins counts across the accepted range.
- **Clipped footprints.** The bound assumes both texels use the full kernel. Where a footprint sample is dropped or moved at a wall (P4), that texel's effective kernel and its pair bound change, Dropping clipped samples and renormalizing keeps the bound at 0.780; moving them to the centre raises it to 0.885. Worse, probes the wall removes break the ring guarantee, so a clipped texel that early-outs on its surviving probes can store a real 0/1 step at the wall's foot (2–5% of edges in simulation). Clipped texels therefore trace their full set (P18).
- **Rejected: reconstruction residual.** The rival detector compares bilinear reconstruction of stored visibility at sub-texel points with freshly traced floored visibility, within a multiple of one over the sample count. A correct round-kernel floor already departs from its own reference by up to 0.14 (0.125 for the tent), whatever the sample count. A point-sampled 45° edge at its best phase departs from the same reference by only 0.20 (0.22 against the tent), and an axis-aligned one by 0.11. No sample-count tolerance separates the two across the allowed counts, and the metric also needs traces, a sample budget and a stride.
