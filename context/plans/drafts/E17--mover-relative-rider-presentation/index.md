# E17--mover-relative-rider-presentation

Brief · resumable · Epic 17 · reads: `context/lib/networking.md` §Current contract, §Host-side remote-pawn presentation, §Snapshot apply ordering · `context/lib/movement.md` §6 (Moving bases) · `context/lib/entity_model.md` §5 · `context/lib/rendering_pipeline.md` (View vs. sim split, kinematic movers) · `plans/done/E17--rotating-movers/` · research: `research.md` · read at 8489a1fe

## Problem
E17 rotating-mover play-tests found co-op riders that do not stay on their platforms. It is an observed defect, seen by players on host and client screens. Cause: a rider and the mover it stands on are drawn at different times or from different poses. Remote riders are drawn at the interpolation delay while their mover is drawn at predicted current time, on a client's screen and on the host's. A snapshot correction also rewrites a mover's current pose without its previous one, so the drawn mover steps under everyone standing on it, the client's own view included. When this is done, every rider stays planted on the platform it stands on from every seat, through spin, reversal and correction. The local eye and pawn are placed through the drawn mover on every seat, so a correction or a fast spin leaves them on the deck. Landing, leaving and changing movers look as they do today.

## Decisions
- **The registry's presented pose is the one render-time mover pose.** Every reader of a mover's drawn pose keeps the registry's render-time accessor (`entity_model.md` §5), so draws, attached lights, ghosts, sound anchors and future readers agree by construction (`research.md` §Where the pose lives). Smoothing is a render-only per-entity correction that accessor applies after its blend; simulation reads of `Transform` never see it. Placement: the entities crate owns the correction as mechanism, client netcode seeds it, and the app advances it once per frame before any presentation consumer.
- **Foot probes on drawn riders read the render-time pose,** through a mover pose source over the presented poses. This diverges from `networking.md` §Host-side remote-pawn presentation, where host-seat foot probes stay on the live end-of-tick mover. Once riders are placed on the render-time pose, a probe against another pose plants feet off the drawn surface. The host-seat rule changes with this brief.
- **Remote riders rebase on both seats.** A connected client's view of every remote player, and the host's view of each connected client's pawn, apply one rule. When both samples that bracket the delayed render time stand on the same mover, the rider's sampled position goes into that mover's frame at the same fractional tick, then out through the mover's presented pose, and is written as the rider's presentation transform as today. Scale and every other orientation term are kept. Neither seat's delay, buffer or teleport-cut rule changes.
- **The historical mover pose comes from authoritative mover history at the rider's own fractional tick.** It is never estimated from the current pose and an elapsed time. A client resolves it from its mover history, whose fixed-tick replay is deterministic. The host resolves it from its own authoritative mover poses over the presentation window. A fractional tick is a blend of the two whole-tick poses around it, the same way the registry blends previous and current poses.
- **The rebase keys on a sampled ground reference, not on entity kind.** The player ground reference already on the wire supplies it, so no wire field, `WIRE_VERSION`, PRL field, FGD key or script surface changes. Roadmap Epic 17 "Co-op presentation of NPCs on moving geometry" then only has to replicate agent ground; it reuses this rebase unchanged.
- **Transitions fall back to world space.** An interval that lands, leaves or changes movers interpolates in world space, as today. The first interval with the same mover on both ends attaches; the first interval whose ends differ detaches. A missing ground sample, missing mover history, an unloaded mover, or a non-finite or non-invertible frame falls back to the sampled world pose. None of these cases panics or keeps a stale attachment.
- **`carry_yaw` turns a remote rider once.** Position always rebases. With the locally loaded mover's `carry_yaw` on, orientation gains the world-up yaw between the historical and render-time mover frames, exactly once per sample. With it off, mover rotation never enters orientation. Pitch and roll never do (`movement.md` §6; `carry_yaw` stays PRL data and never crosses the wire, per `plans/done/E17--rotating-movers/`).
- **Remote locomotion is unchanged.** Animation and lower-body heading keep reading replicated intrinsic velocity (`plans/done/E17--rotating-movers/`, Remote rider presentation).
- **Mover corrections are smoothed, render-only, on connected clients.** A snapshot correction leaves the drawn mover where it was drawn the frame before, then converges on the corrected pose over presented time, at a rate independent of frame rate. Only the presented pose reads the smoothing state. It clears on mover despawn, level unload, mover rebind, non-finite input, and a correction at or above the pawn reconcile's teleport-class floor (`networking.md` §Current contract, reconcile); that last case snaps.
- **The local eye rides the drawn deck on every seat.** This covers single-player, the host's own pawn and a connected client's predicted pawn. When both fixed-tick endpoints stand on the same mover, the pawn's capsule centre is blended in that mover's frame and placed through the mover's presented pose. Eye height is then added along world-up, so mover pitch and roll never tilt the eye. The camera eye and the local pawn mesh (shadow and mirror) take the result. Any other case keeps today's world-space blend. The existing render-yaw residual keeps owning yaw, and with `carry_yaw` on, the view yaw also takes the presented correction's world-up yaw. One rule serves both the arc between ticks and a smoothed correction (`research.md` §Local eye).
- **A mover correction reaches the client's eye once.** Pawn corrections already seed the world-space presentation offset, and a mover correction is also a correction to the pawn standing on it. While the pawn stands on the same mover before and after, the share of its correction the mover correction explains reaches the eye through the presented pose only. It is never also seeded into the pawn offset. The rest, from mispredicted input or a hold, keeps the world-space offset, which the mover never rotates. When the pawn leaves that mover, the mover correction still at its point moves into its world-space offset, so the eye never steps.
- **Presentation never writes authority.** Pawn movement, ground reference, mover phase, collision poses, mover history and outgoing snapshots are never written by any of this. Non-riders, static bases, transform-only remotes and maps without movers present as today.
- **Non-goals:**
  - NPC riders: the roadmap item above owns them, and they need a wire bump this brief rules out.
  - Delaying predicted movers to the remote-actor time: it would bring back input lag on the client's own platform.
  - Arc interpolation for NPCs riding movers on single-player and the host: they keep the straight blend, whose gap is millimetres at authored spin rates (`research.md` §Local eye). The roadmap NPC item above owns NPC riders.
  - Camera pitch/roll carry, angular momentum, nested movers, scale carry, and general temporal smoothing for other entities.

## Acceptance
### Automated
**Remote riders stay planted:**
- [ ] On the rotating-platform fixture under the mandated conditioned-link profile, a stationary remote rider on a mover that rotates and translates stays at its mover-local point within `1e-4` m of the drawn mover for at least ten revolutions, with no accumulating drift. This holds on a connected client viewing the host and on the host viewing the client.
- [ ] Spin start, stop and reversal mid-interval keep the same bound.
- [ ] With `carry_yaw` on, a remote rider's yaw equals its sampled yaw plus the historical-to-render-time world-up mover yaw. Sampling the same frame twice gives the same yaw. With `carry_yaw` off, mover rotation adds zero yaw. A mover pitch or roll leaves the rider's orientation untouched in both cases.
- [ ] An interval whose bracketing samples name the same mover attaches, and one whose samples differ (landing, leaving, mover to mover) draws exactly today's world-space pose. Both sides are tested.
- [ ] Ground history survives sparse deltas: Transform-only and movement-only samples each attach when the latest ground at or before their tick names the mover.
- [ ] Missing ground, missing or evicted mover history, an unloaded mover and a non-finite mover frame each draw the sampled world pose, without panic or a stale attachment on the next interval.
- [ ] A remote rider's teleport generation change cuts as today; the first interval after the cut attaches only once its two samples name one mover.
- [ ] A planted foot on a rebased rider keeps its hit and its model-space contact height within `1e-3` across snapshot arrivals and render alphas, on both seats.

**Corrections are smooth and counted once:**
- [ ] On the frame a mover correction is applied, at any alpha and on a frame that runs zero fixed ticks, the drawn mover moves at most `1e-4` m and `1e-4` rad from the previous frame's drawn pose beyond its own motion. A synthetic correction converges to the corrected pose without accumulating drift, in the same presented time at 30, 60 and 240 Hz rendering.
- [ ] Before and after the change, the harness records the worst same-frame visual step for the conditioned rotating-platform run and the synthetic zero-tick correction. The before figure shows the step this brief removes.
- [ ] Two corrections to one mover on one frame compose; a correction at or above the teleport-class floor snaps; despawn, level unload, rebind and non-finite input clear the smoothing.
- [ ] A connected client's pawn standing on a corrected mover keeps its eye within `1e-4` m of its point on the drawn deck from the correction frame to the end of the glide. With `carry_yaw` on, its view yaw follows the drawn mover's yaw with no step. A correction from mispredicted input on the same frame still glides through the world-space offset.
- [ ] A pawn that jumps off, or steps onto another mover, while a correction is mid-glide moves its eye by no more than its own motion on that frame, and lands on its world position by the end of the glide.
- [ ] Single-player, the host's own pawn and a client pawn on static ground or airborne draw an eye and pawn mesh bit-equal to before.

**Local eye rides the deck:**
- [ ] On single-player, the host's own pawn and a connected client, a pawn standing on a mover that turns a quarter turn in one tick is drawn at the arc midpoint at alpha 0.5, not the chord midpoint. The eye's distance from the rotation axis stays constant within `1e-4` m across alphas.
- [ ] With the mover pitched or rolled, the eye sits exactly eye height above the placed capsule centre along world-up.
- [ ] Landing, leaving, mover change, fly-camera, menu camera, teleport hold and a missing pawn each draw exactly today's world-space blend. The frame after any of them carries no attachment from before.
- [ ] On a frame where smoothing is active, the mover's beauty, shadow, occluder, attached light, ghost and sound-anchor poses, the remote rider rebase, the foot probe and the client's eye all agree with the mover's presented pose. A test feeds a correction and checks each against it.

**Authority and scope:**
- [ ] With rebasing and smoothing active, pawn positions, ground references, mover phases, collision poses, mover history and serialized snapshots are bit-equal to a run with both disabled.
- [ ] `WIRE_VERSION` and the snapshot layout are unchanged.
- [ ] A rebased remote rider's locomotion animation and lower-body heading equal those of the same run without rebasing.
- [ ] Non-riders, riders on static ground, transform-only remote entities and a mover-less map draw bit-equal to before.
- [ ] A fixture with every remote pawn riding, across several movers at once, keeps every bound above. Rebasing and smoothing allocate nothing per frame in steady state.

### Manual
- [ ] Host and client ride the kinematic-platform carousel together under the dev lag proxy at the harness profile, facing each other. Through spin, reversal, jumping off and landing back on, each sees the other planted with feet on the deck and a steady view, and no correction pop. A recipe for this lands in the map's README.

## Path
- Seams: `EntityRegistry::interpolated_transform` and `set_presentation_transform` (`crates/entities/src/registry.rs`); `ClientReplication::sample_into_registry` and `RemoteInterpolationBuffer::presented_pose` (client remotes); `host_presentation::present_client_pawns`, `probe_presented_client_pawn_feet` (host seat); `MoverHistoryBuffer` and `client/replay_poses.rs` replay (client history); `ClientReplication::apply_components_to`, `prepare_mover_apply`, `MoverCorrection`, `apply_authoritative_mover_corrections` (corrections); `reconcile.rs` ordinary-correction arm and `seed_presentation_offset_with_life_change` (own-pawn share); `FrameTiming`, `InterpolableState`, every `push_state`/`hold_state` caller, `follow_camera_to_local_pawn`, `effective_render_yaw`, `mover_yaw_carry_ground`, `MeshRenderCollector::collect_inner` (local eye and pawn); `KinematicMoverRenderCollector::collect_with_mirror`, `light_bridge.rs`, `ghost_presented_transform`, `sound_events/anchors.rs` (consumers that follow by construction); `MoverPoseSource` (foot-probe adapter).
- Chosen shape: a sparse render-only correction keyed by entity, applied inside `interpolated_transform`, plus a `MoverPoseSource` adapter that answers mover poses from it at the frame alpha. Strongest rival: an App-owned per-frame mover pose table handed to listed consumers. It caches each pose once, but every unlisted reader draws a different mover while smoothing runs.
- Own-pawn share: the mover-explained share is the mover correction's displacement of the pawn's pre-correction point, when ground names the same mover before and after.
- First slice: the client-view remote rebase on the rotating fixture, measured against the ten-revolution row. It tests fractional history sampling, the riskiest assumption.
- Split first, behaviour-preserving, own commits: `crates/netcode/src/client.rs` (remote presentation and mover history out), `crates/postretro/src/main.rs` (camera follow and yaw carry helpers out), `crates/sim/src/sim/mod.rs` (foot probing out). `interpolation.rs`, `mesh_render.rs` and `runtime_movers.rs` are past 800 lines but mostly tests; add at their boundaries.
