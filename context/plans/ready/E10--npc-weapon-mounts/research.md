## NPC shot origin
Owner-observed 2026-11-23 on `movement-feel`: the `rifle_trooper` (white armor, `rodin_sci-fi_trooper_m`) fires from above its head. Pre-existing: `enemy_eye` dates from `3d45c494e` (2026-08-23, enemy static LOS fire gate), and E21 did not touch it.

Today:
- An NPC shot launches from the AI eye. `WeaponAim.origin` is `perception.enemy_eye` (`crates/ai/src/compute.rs`, the fire proposal). The AI weapon controller launches the projectile at `aim.origin` (`AiWeaponControllers::advance_actor`, `weapon_controller.rs`).
- The eye is the top of the hitbox. `enemy_eye` (`crates/ai/src/perception.rs`) returns `position + hitbox.offset + Y * hitbox.half_extents.y` when the health block declares a hitbox. Without one, it falls back to `EYE_FACTOR` (0.9) × the baked nav agent height. The branches disagree: the hitbox branch applies no eye factor.
- Line of sight reads the same point (`perception.rs`).
- The AI path never calls `resolve_muzzle_launch_origin` or `muzzle_world_origin` (`crates/sim/src/weapon/mod.rs`). Those compose a player's shot from first-person placement plus `muzzleOffset`, fall back to the eye when the muzzle is obstructed, and converge on the aim target. No enemy rifle declares `muzzleOffset` (`content/dev/scripts/enemy-rifle.ts`), and adding one would change nothing.
- `resolve_muzzle_launch_origin` takes the placement and a model-local muzzle and composes the world muzzle itself. Its obstruction test is the eye-to-muzzle sweep at the projectile's radius, plus the eye ray reaching world at or before the muzzle's forward plane. An NPC caller already holds a world muzzle point.

Measured 2026-11-23:

| Archetype | Hitbox half-height and offset (y) | Shot origin | Head top | Hand and gun |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `rifle_trooper` | 1.0, 1.0 | 2.00 m | 1.88–1.91 m | 1.5–1.6 m |
| `rifle_warrior` | 0.95, 0.95 | 1.90 m | ~1.80 m | not measured |
| `limitator` | 0.91, 0.91 | 1.82 m | ~1.74 m | not measured |

The enemy projectile sprite is 0.55 m (`enemyRifleProjectile`, `enemy-rifle.ts`), so it draws mostly above the head.

Design smells (owner-agreed):
1. The eye derives from the hitbox, a damage volume. A gameplay hitbox change moves where an NPC sees from.
2. Sight origin and shot origin are one point. Line of sight and cover are a different concern from weapon launch.
3. Launch origin branches by actor kind: player projectiles go through `resolve_muzzle_launch_origin`, NPCs bypass it. That runs against `entity_model.md` §2's one weapon vocabulary, which has no player-vs-enemy branch.

Directions weighed (the owner chose the held weapon's muzzle on 2026-11-23, with a built-in weapon's muzzle as a body socket; a later ruling dropped the eye fallback, §Blocked muzzle):
- A separate NPC launch point. The eye stays for line of sight. The launch point is one of:
  - an authored chest or shoulder offset in the facing frame;
  - the held weapon's muzzle: hand joint × rig bake × the bake's rigid `muzzle` socket.
- Separately, make the eye an authored height or a factor-scaled one, so a hitbox edit stops moving it.

Costs of the held-muzzle source:
- Reading the held mount for gameplay reverses E21's Decision "Gameplay never reads a mount" (`networking.md` §Weapon placement is content) and `resource_management.md` §7's presentation-only sockets, for host-only NPCs.
- Aim drift with clip pose: a muzzle read from the posed hand moves with every clip. For scale, Mixamo rifle clips hold the hand-to-hand line 24° or more off the +Z mount (measured 2026-11-05: `idle_aiming` 24°, `firing_rifle` 38°).

## Blocked muzzle
Owner ruling, 2026-11-23, after `/validate-plan` returned Under-scoped: a muzzle whose path to the target is blocked whiffs; the shot never launches from the eye.
- The eye gate: `line_of_sight_combined` from `enemy_eye` to `target_aim`, world and movers at the tick's pose (`perception.rs`). `perceive_target` debounces it through the LOS grace (`debounce_los`), and `fire_gate` reads the debounced verdict.
- `line_of_sight_combined` tests the exact segment, with no radius (`crates/physics/src/collision/shot_queries.rs`). A projectile sweeps at its radius, so a lip below the muzzle-to-target segment but within the radius of it passes that test and still catches the shot.
- The pawn precedent, `resolve_muzzle_launch_origin` (`crates/sim/src/weapon/mod.rs`), sweeps eye to muzzle at `projectile_radius` through `cast_sphere_exact_combined` (a segment test at radius 0), and also counts the eye ray reaching world at or before the muzzle's forward distance as obstructed. A pawn's obstruction falls back to the eye; an NPC's whiffs.
- The fire proposal builds `WeaponAim` whenever target perception exists, visible or not (`compute.rs`). `advance_actor` cancels an execution only when the aim is absent, and each due shot launches at that tick's aim. So a running burst keeps firing after sight is lost.
- `enemy_eye_height_clears_low_cover_but_full_cover_blocks_fire` (`crates/ai/src/ai_tests.rs`): a meshless NPC with a hitbox half-height 1.5 at offset 0, so its eye is 1.5 m; the pawn 2 m away, eye 0.5 m; walls at x = 1, 0.9 m (the eye ray crosses at 1.0 m, so it fires) and 1.25 m (blocked). Its attack is contact damage (8 HP, applied in-tick), not a projectile, so it proves the eye gate only.
- The band the ruling accepts: cover between gun height and the hitbox top, near the NPC, passes the eye gate and blocks the muzzle. The measured eyes sit 0.08–0.12 m above the head top, and the trooper's 0.4–0.5 m above its gun (§NPC shot origin). An eye at head height would narrow it; the eye's derivation stays out of scope.
- Rivals weighed: launch from the muzzle into the cover (the NPC shoots the lip in front of it); fall back to the eye as a pawn does (restores the defect); the chest or shoulder offset (the owner's earlier rejection: off the drawn gun); a zero-radius muzzle-to-target segment (a lip within the shot's radius passes it and catches the shot); test only the burst's first shot, so later shots launch from the muzzle as the weapon's steps time them (a later shot flies into cover that rose or that the NPC turned into).
- Foreclosure flagged by validation: once the muzzle socket is gameplay, art edits are gameplay edits. Re-baking an AR_4 (its scale, seat or solve) or moving its `muzzle` node moves the shot start and changes which cover blocks the NPC. `solve-weapon-mount --check` measures orientation only (`resource_management.md` §7).

- Owner ruling, 2026-11-23: a burst shot skipped for a blocked muzzle path spends the weapon's ammo, heat or cell as if it fired. The owner's reason: part of the fun of shooters is outsmarting enemies, tricking them into wasting shots on columns or on each other.
- Design tension, settled by the owner after the third validation (§Validation history input): a full-path muzzle test made an NPC hold fire rather than shoot a column the player ducks behind, while the owner values tricking enemies into shooting columns. Only the near path now holds a shot.

## Fire decision
Read at dcd60bfb3, after the third `/validate-plan`.
- One shared verdict: `done/E10--enemy-line-of-sight-cover/` made a single per-tick, loss-grace debounced eye verdict that `targetVisible` and the fire gate both read. The SDK doc comment says so ("the LOS verdict the engine fire gate also reads"); it originates in the typedef templates (`crates/scripting-core/src/typedef/templates/sdk_lib.d.ts`, `.luau`) and is generated into `sdk/types/`. `fire_gate_projects_the_shared_perception_verdict` (`crates/ai/src/perception.rs`) pins it.
- `attacksFiredInActivity`: "Successful attack fires since the currently-evaluated activity was entered", visible on the next tick's guard refresh (SDK doc). It is `Brain.attacks_fired_in_activity`, an array per nesting depth with a serde default, host-only, not in snapshot payloads (`crates/entities/src/components/brain.rs`), projected through `ScopeRelativeValues` (`crates/ai/src/brain_scope.rs`) and filled from `activity_attack_count` in `graph_eval.rs` and `compute.rs`.
- The shipped combat-slot scorer keeps a candidate, reposition's ring slots included, only when the eye sees the target from it: `slot_sees_target(position + enemy_eye_offset)` in `crates/sim/src/combat_positioning/scoring.rs`, reached from `ring_candidates` (`rotated_ring.rs`). Its comment calls slot visibility a positioning heuristic, with the fire gate authoritative. An earlier reading here (no sight test, from `crates/ai/src/engaged_motion.rs` alone) was false; review round 1 caught it.
- `drafts/E10--enemy-cover/`: a `half` point qualifies when the target cannot see the crouched eye there but can see the standing eye. Its example peek leaves on `attacksFiredInActivity.ge(1)` after 900 ms, or on `timeInActivityMs.ge(1500)`, so a held peek still leaves by timer; the held fact lets it leave on the hold.
- Rivals weighed for the blocked muzzle after this validation: keep the full-path hold and add the AI-facing half (the cost the validation named, and the column tension stays); fire into cover with only the body-to-muzzle test (an NPC shoots its own cover's lip); hold only on near cover (the owner's choice). For the held signal: a fact, or timers only (the owner chose the fact). For cover scoring: decide the rule here, or split a brief on when an NPC fires (the owner kept it here, as this brief already owns the fire decision).
- A flinch already cancels a burst in flight: apply calls `cancel_actor` when `outcome.flinched` (`crates/ai/src/apply.rs`), and attack-turn arbitration cancels one whose turn ends or whose attack no longer resolves (`attack_turns/lifecycle.rs`). A script reaction that switches the drawn state without a flinch does not; `advance_actor` cancels only on an absent aim (`weapon_controller.rs`). A clip moment already fires nothing after a foreign switch (`entity_model.md` §7c, One chance per entry).
- The clip clock tracks no state when the path's activity draws no animation: `sync_drawn_state` clears `state` when `drawn_state` returns none (`crates/ai/src/clip_clock.rs`).
- The brain's own switches happen in apply only on a state change, a stride change, a launch clip, a travel-variant change or a redraw (`crates/ai/src/apply.rs`); a script reaction (`reactions/animation.rs` → `setAnimationState`) stays drawn until one of those. A failed own switch (`UnknownState`, clip unresolved) warns once and keeps the prior animation while `sync_drawn_state` tracks the new state. A flinch cancels a burst only where its activity routes flinches (`staggerTo`). A pending redraw holds the clock and defers a clip moment rather than spending it.
- An impact `playAnim` on a living enemy already froze it on the reaction's last frame: `limitator.ts` routes its hit reaction through a graph `flinch` activity for that reason, and content uses `playAnim` otherwise only for death. The stagger (`stagger`, `stagger_due` in `crates/ai/src/clip_clock.rs`) times a foreign switch only while an activity on the path routes flinches (`staggerTo`), capped at `DEFAULT_RECOVERY_MS` in game time. Each animation state already takes an authored `playbackRate` (`sdk/types/postretro.d.ts`; `reference-enemy.ts` sets 2.5), and a reaction drawn at a slower rate ends its clip later in game time while the stagger's cap stays game time (`begin_tick`'s doc, `clip_clock.rs`). An earlier reading here (no authored rate) was false; review round 3 caught it.
- Rivals weighed after the fourth validation: a muzzle posed once at load per archetype and attack state, kept as a facing-relative offset (drops NM-1 and NM-2; a looping clip's pose drifts from one offset by an unmeasured amount); drawing the projectile from the drawn muzzle while the gameplay origin stays on the body (`done/weapon-muzzle-origin/` rejected it for pawns over client validation, which NPCs lack; it still loses here, since the drawn shot would cross the cover lip). For a runtime gap: the last good muzzle, the eye at head height through `EYE_FACTOR`, or accepting the eye; the owner chose neither and ruled that an NPC fires only while aiming.

## Muzzle pose
- The clip clock is `ClipClock` (`crates/entities/src/components/brain/clip_clock.rs`; rules in `crates/ai/src/clip_clock.rs`). `state` is the drawn animation state with its travel-variant suffix; `time` is unwrapped clip time, each tick's `dt × rate` from the state's start or drawn phase. `begin_tick` advances it for every brain on every tick the NPC acts, not only for clip-timed attacks. `state` is `None` when it tracks nothing.
- `HitZoneStore::posed_socket_world` (`crates/sim/src/scripting_systems/hit_zones.rs`) samples a body socket on demand through `sample_world_pose_for_probe`. That resolves `MeshAnimation` stamps at an animation-clock `anim_time`, and returns `None` while the current state's clip is unresolved. Its callers (damaged-enemy overlay, client markers, netcode presentation) pass presentation time. So a pose at clip time samples the drawn clip at `ClipClock.time` directly: a new entry, with no crossfade blend.
- It does not compose an attachment's own socket. A bake's rigid `muzzle` socket is a rest transform in the bake's mesh frame (`resource_management.md` §7), composed on the posed holder joint as the renderer places the attachment.
- Gameplay capsules and probes omit presentation-only pose modifiers (`entity_model.md` §7, Skeletal hit zones). The muzzle pose follows that rule.
- The clock tracks nothing while `state` is `None`. A moved `mesh_switches` count is a switch the brain did not make (a script reaction or impact effect), and `redraw` marks a fresh mesh not yet redrawn (a held enemy's drop): the clock holds until the redraw. On such a tick the mesh draws a state the clock does not time, so no clip-time pose of the drawn state exists.

## Mounts today
- Third-person mounts are character-owned (E21). A character's `mesh.weaponMounts` maps each weapon to socket → model attachments baked for its rig (`resource_management.md` §7). `WeaponDescriptor.third_person_model` is retired.
- The entry type, `WeaponMountDescriptor` (`crates/entities/src/data_descriptors/types/weapon_mounts.rs`), holds `weapon` and an `attachments` map, not optional. `validate_weapon_mounts` rejects an empty map ("an entry mounts at least one socket"), and `check_weapon_mount_key` rejects any key outside `WEAPON_MOUNT_KEYS` (`weapon`, `attachments`). No entry field names a muzzle today.
- The mount writer, `update_weapon_mount_attachments` (`crates/netcode/src/weapon_mounts.rs`), composes base `mesh.attachments`, then the one active weapon's entry, through `compose_mounted_attachments`. It runs for pawns only. NPCs mount their rifle through `mesh.attachments`: the limitator directly, and the rifle squads by spreading its `mesh` and replacing `hand_r` (`rifle-squads.ts`).
- The limitator's attacks fire two weapons: `enemy_rifle_burst` (`shoot`, `spray`) and `enemy_rifle_long` (`longshot`, `content/dev/scripts/limitator.ts`). One `hand_r` attachment, `models/ar_4/limitator.gltf`, shows both. The rifle squads spread the limitator, keeping its attacks, and replace that attachment with their own bake.
- E21's install check, `check_weapon_mount_tables` (`crates/sim/src/scripting_systems/weapon_mounts.rs`), already walks NPC tables and warns for an entry whose weapon name resolves to no weapon.
- The level-install sweep and model prediction already collect every descriptor's `weaponMounts` models, NPCs included (`weapon_presentation_models`, `data_archetype.rs`).
- `descriptor_mesh_component` (`data_archetype.rs`) builds an NPC's `MeshComponent` both on host attach and on client materialize (`materialize_armed_remote_enemy` → `materialize_net_descriptor_presentation`, `net_descriptor.rs`). An NPC's snapshot identity is `entity_class = "descriptor:<canonical_name>"` (`crates/netcode/src/descriptor_class.rs`), so a client resolves the same descriptor by name.
- E21 re-seated the AR_4 trooper and warrior bakes (`content/dev/models/ar_4/`). The warrior's rifle moved out of its torso. The trooper's bake scaled 1.41×, to 0.859 m, so its support hand reaches the handguard: the rig's hands sit ~0.54 m apart, wider than the stock AR_4's 0.29 m from grip to muzzle. Content scale standards are on the roadmap under Epic 21.
- No AR_4 bake carries a node socket (read at 73b072de3: no node `extras` in any of the three). The cyberpunk viewmodels do: the rifle's `model.gltf` has a `muzzle` node with `extras.socket = "muzzle"`.

## Handed to the pose brief
After the fifth `/validate-plan` returned Reshape (2026-11-23), the owner moved pose ownership out of this brief into the pose-and-stagger brief, which also covers authored stagger kinds and later supplies the aiming classification. This brief keeps the fire-step rule and reads that brief's interruption state. What moves, as the owner ruled it here, for that brief to adopt or revisit:

Decision, as adopted during review rounds 2–4:
- **The brain owns a living enemy's drawn state.** A switch the brain did not make, such as a script reaction's `playAnim` or `setAnimationState`, is a flinch: the whole stagger an impact flinch takes today (`entity_model.md` §7c, Release exit), generalized from activities that route flinches to every brain. In an activity with no flinch route, the enemy is planted, a burst in flight cancels unspent, only an activity entry or the stagger's end draws over the reaction, and its end redraws the tracked state; on the tick the end is due, the stagger clears in compute before the fire proposal, as a route's entry clears it. A foreign switch that apply sees before this enemy's launch, such as a reaction an earlier enemy's contact hit ran, starts the stagger there. An entry into an activity that draws no animation does not end it; at its end the brain draws the mesh's default state. A death ends the brain, so a death clip stays; on revival, a stagger running at death is dropped and the recovery's rest switch counts as the brain's own; graph state, counts and cooldowns carry over as today. The owner's reason: gameplay now reads the pose, so the pose has one owner, and an impact `playAnim` on a living enemy already froze on its last frame (`research.md` §Fire decision). A lasting state on a living enemy belongs in its behavior graph. Pre-stable, `setAnimationState`'s contract on a living enemy changes with no alias.

Non-goals this brief carried:
  - More than one stagger kind, such as a subtle flinch that keeps moving and firing, or a knockdown like a fall to sitting: its own brief, which decides per kind whether the enemy keeps moving and firing, and so where its muzzle reads. Here every reaction is today's one flinch.
  - A lasting pose a script triggers on a living enemy, such as a taunt or a cutscene pose: it now plays as a flinch and ends at the stagger's end. A route for it is later work; the behavior graph holds lasting states today.

Rows this brief carried:
- [ ] The same reaction mid-burst, in an activity with no flinch route, is a flinch: the burst in flight cancels with its owed shots unspent, the enemy stays planted until the stagger ends, and after the redraw the next attack fires from the muzzle. `attacksHeldInActivity` does not change.
